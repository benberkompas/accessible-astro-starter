Right now, we require even young children to be added as authenticated users. If they don't have an email address, they use a parent's. That’s sensible, but it means we can't enforce unique emails at sign-up — otherwise parents couldn’t use their email address more than once. 

Because we don’t enforce unique email address at sign-up, some people end up with duplicate accounts they never intended to create. 

The whole idea behind having parents add each child as a user was to help us attribute activity correctly. If the child is signed in to a device as a particular user, we can say any activity that occurs is likely to be theirs.

There’s a cascade of assumptions hiding here. 

1. If a child is too young to have their own email address, are they likely to know their own username and password?
2. If they don’t know those, who will have to sign in for them? The parent. 
3. And if multiple children share a device, the likelihood of anyone remembering to switch users — especially on an app designed to run invisibly in the background — is essentially zero.

I modeled an alternative: make device is its own object.

<FramedImage
  src="/projects/orca-navigator/placeholder.jpg"
  alt="Placeholder"
  caption="Placeholder"
/>

This would mean:

- A child's iPad can be named, associated with that child, and the device itself can be used for attribution — without requiring the child to exist as an authenticated user in the system.
- The email reuse scenario goes away.
- The duplicate accounts go away.
- The attribution problem gets a clean solution.

I've presented this to our member care team and engineering leadership, and it's gaining traction largely because I’m framing it as a system-level structural change.
